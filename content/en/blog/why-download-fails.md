# Why a Facebook Reel Download May Fail and How to Troubleshoot It

You pasted a link, pressed Download, and got an error instead of a file. It is frustrating, but there is almost always an explanation. This article goes through the messages you may see, what each one really means, and what you can do. Sometimes the honest answer is that nothing can be done for that particular video, and knowing that saves time.

## Start with the three-minute checklist

Before digging into details, try these in order:

1. **Copy the link again** with **Share → Copy link** inside Facebook, rather than reusing an old or shortened version.
2. **Make sure the video is public.** Open the link in a private (incognito) browser window where you are not logged in. If you cannot watch it there, it is not public.
3. **Check for extra text.** The box should contain only the link. Remove anything before or after it.
4. **Wait a minute and retry** once, not ten times in a row.
5. **Try a different browser or device** if the page itself behaves strangely.

If these do not solve it, find your message below.

## "This link is not supported" or "invalid link"

The tool accepts links that point to Facebook (facebook.com, m.facebook.com, web.facebook.com or fb.watch) and that look like a Reel or video. It rejects links to other sites, links to a profile or a photo, and links with missing pieces.

**What to do:** go back to the Reel or video itself, tap **Share → Copy link**, and paste that. Do not paste a screenshot, a page title, or a link to your feed.

## "Private or inaccessible content"

This means the video is not publicly viewable. It might be friends-only, in a private group, restricted by age or location, or visible only to certain audiences.

**What to do:** nothing, from the tool's side. We do not bypass privacy settings, and no legitimate tool should. If it is your own video, use your original file or the download options in your account or Page tools. If it belongs to someone else, ask the owner to send you the file or to make a copy available.

## "Video unavailable"

The video may have been deleted, the owner may have changed its privacy, or the link may be incomplete or broken. Reels can also disappear after being reported or removed.

**What to do:** open the link in a browser. If Facebook itself says the content is unavailable, there is nothing to download. If it plays normally while you are logged out, copy the link again and retry.

## "No downloadable format is available"

The video was found, but there is no file the tool can offer for it. This can happen with some live videos, with videos that use special protection, or with videos whose files are not exposed for download.

**What to do:** retry later in case it was temporary. If it keeps happening, that video cannot be downloaded with this tool. Do not try to get around it with suspicious sites.

## "Service temporarily unavailable"

The video service that the site relies on could not answer, was busy, or returned something unexpected. It is usually temporary.

**What to do:** wait a few minutes and try again. If the message persists for a long time, the problem is on the service side, not yours.

## "Too many requests"

To protect the service from abuse, each visitor can only make a limited number of requests in a short time. If you press Download many times quickly, or share a network with many other users, you may hit the limit.

**What to do:** wait for the number of seconds shown, then try once.

## "The request took too long"

A timeout means the video service did not respond in time. It could be a slow moment, a very busy period, or a problem reaching the video.

**What to do:** try again after a short pause. If you are on a weak mobile connection, move to Wi-Fi.

## "The download link has expired"

Download links are short-lived on purpose, around 15 minutes, so that links cannot be shared around or reused later. If you left the page open and came back, the link will have expired.

**What to do:** press **Download** again on the video to get a fresh link, then save immediately.

## "Downloading is not available on this site right now"

This means the site's video service has not been configured by its operator. It is not caused by your link or device.

**What to do:** check back later. If you operate the site, see the configuration instructions in the project documentation.

## When the page works but the file will not save

Sometimes the result appears, but tapping **Save** does nothing or the file does not show up.

- **Browser blocks the download.** Check pop-up and download settings, and allow downloads from this site.
- **Storage is full.** Free up space on your device.
- **A content blocker interferes.** Temporarily pause it for this site and try again.
- **You cannot find the file.** On Android look in the Files app under **Downloads**; on iPhone look in **Files → Downloads** (see the [iPhone guide](blog:save-facebook-reels-iphone)); on a computer check the browser's download list.
- **The file will not play.** Choose another quality option if one is offered, or open it with a different player.

## What to avoid

- **Never enter your Facebook password, cookies or tokens** on any downloader. A tool for public videos does not need them.
- **Do not install random apps or browser extensions** because a page says they will "fix" the download.
- **Do not keep hammering the button.** It triggers limits and makes things worse.

## Why a video that worked before might fail today

Facebook changes how its pages and files work from time to time. A video that was downloadable yesterday can stop being available if its owner changes its privacy, deletes it, or if the platform changes how the file is delivered. This is outside any tool's control, which is also why no website can honestly promise that every video will always work.
