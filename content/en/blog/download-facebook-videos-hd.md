# How to Download Supported Facebook Videos in HD (When HD Exists)

Everyone would like the sharpest copy of a video. But "download in HD" is not a button you can press for every Facebook video, and any site that promises HD for everything is promising something it cannot control. This guide explains what actually decides the quality you can get, how to check what is available for a particular video, and how to avoid common mistakes.

## What "HD" and "SD" really mean

Online video comes in several versions, often called renditions. A platform takes the file you upload and creates smaller versions so that it can play smoothly on slow connections and small screens.

- **SD (standard definition)** is a lower-resolution version, often somewhere around 360p or 480p. It is smaller and loads quickly.
- **HD (high definition)** is a higher-resolution version, commonly 720p or 1080p. It is larger and looks sharper on big screens.

The labels a download tool shows are usually the labels it received for each file. They are approximate descriptions, not guarantees about how the video will look.

## What decides the quality you can get

**1. The original upload.** A platform cannot create detail that was never in the file. If someone uploaded a 480p clip, there is no real HD version to download, and a "720p" label on such a file would be misleading. Resolution cannot be improved after the fact; upscaling only makes pixels bigger, not sharper.

**2. How the platform processed it.** Facebook creates different versions of each video. Which versions exist, and for how long, is Facebook's decision. For Reels the picture is vertical (9:16), so a "1080p" Reel is 1080 pixels wide and 1920 tall, while a horizontal 1080p video is the other way around.

**3. What is exposed to a downloader.** Even when several versions exist, only some of them may be reachable for a given video. Some videos expose just one file.

**4. Whether the video is public.** Private, friends-only or restricted videos cannot be processed at all, in any quality.

**5. The service connected to the downloader.** The tool relies on a video service to find downloadable files. That service decides what it can read from each public video.

## How to see what is available for a video

1. Copy the video's link with **Share → Copy link** in the Facebook app, or copy the address from your browser.
2. Paste it into [the downloader](page:home) and press **Download**.
3. Read the list that appears. Each line shows a type (video or audio), a quality label and, when known, a format and size.

Here is the key point: __BRAND__ only shows qualities that actually exist for that video. If you see an HD line, HD is available. If you see only SD, then SD is what exists, and no setting will change that.

## Choosing between HD and SD

HD is not always the right choice.

- **Choose HD** for videos you will watch on a large screen, edit, or keep as an archive of your own work.
- **Choose SD** when storage or mobile data is limited, for sending over messaging apps with size limits, or for videos that will only ever be watched on a phone.

As a rough guide, a short Reel is often only a few megabytes in SD and may be several times larger in HD. When the file size is known, the results list shows it so you can decide before saving.

## If you only see one option

That is common, and it usually has a simple reason: the video was uploaded at modest quality, or only one file is available for it. Try these checks:

- Make sure you copied the original video's link, not a re-share of a lower-quality copy.
- Wait a little and check again. A very recent video may not have all its versions ready yet.
- If the video is yours, use your original file from your phone or computer. That will always be better than any copy that passed through a platform. If you manage a Page, your Page tools (for example Meta Business Suite) may let you download your uploads, and the "Download your information" option in account settings can export a copy of your own data. Menu names change over time.

## What not to do

- **Do not trust claims of "4K" or "original quality" for every video.** If the source was not 4K, there is no 4K to download.
- **Do not use sites that ask for your Facebook password, cookies or login.** A legitimate downloader for public videos never needs them, and sharing them can lead to account takeover.
- **Do not install unknown "downloader" apps or extensions** just to get HD. Many are unreliable, and some are harmful.
- **Do not upscale and call it HD.** Upscaling makes a file bigger, not better.

## Playing and checking the file you got

After saving, open the file and look at its properties. On a computer you can usually right-click and view details to see the resolution. On a phone, the Files or Photos app often shows it in an info panel. If you picked HD and the result looks soft, the original was probably not very sharp to begin with.

If the file will not play on your phone, try another player app, or choose a different option next time. Common formats like MP4 are widely supported; some web formats are not.

## Using videos responsibly

Quality is only part of the picture. Even a perfect HD copy does not give you the right to re-upload, sell or reuse someone else's video. Save content you own, content that is offered for reuse, or content you have permission for. ## Quick recap

1. HD exists only if the original and the platform provide it.
2. The tool shows only qualities that really exist for that video.
3. One option is normal; it is not a bug.
4. For your own videos, your original file beats any download.
5. Never share your Facebook login with a downloader.
